# Rolling Commons: Community-Ground Scope and Service Boundaries

## Purpose

Community grounds are a voluntary, dignity-supporting pathway for people and
households to build or maintain viable mobile/demountable living, practical
skills, mutual aid, and shared infrastructure. They respond to material conditions—rent escalation,
shared mortgages or debt, deposits and contracts, recurring service costs,
relationship or disaster transitions, local ties, and places with little basic
infrastructure. They are intentionally designed as an alternative to dangerous
or isolating survival—not as a system that requires someone to become
criminalised or endure violence before receiving stability and support.

The simulation must never turn housing insecurity into a shameful identity or a
test of personal value. A person does not become less worthy because an extractive
market, a contract, a platform charge, or a relationship change limits their
choices. The design question is what material capability a place and its network
can build together.

They are **not** a replacement for social/public housing, specialist supported
housing, auspiced services, aged care, disability support, hospital care,
inpatient mental-health services, domestic/family violence services, alcohol and
other drug treatment, or other wraparound support. Those settings may be needed
temporarily or long term. The game must not suggest that a community ground can
meet every need, or that a person is deficient if one configuration is not right
for their present circumstances.

## Mobility-centred participation scope

Some community-ground scenarios depend on members being able to participate in a
vehicle/mobile-dwelling model and maintain the pack-defined responsibilities for
that model. A scenario may therefore require a demonstrated **simulated
mobility-readiness/road-use responsibility** for ordinary participation, because
vehicle movement, maintenance, site access, shared safety, and peer operations
are central to that particular environment.

This is a ground-configuration rule, not a judgement about a person. It must never use a
health condition, disability, age, trauma history, criminal record, body size,
or a raw real-world licence status as a hidden proxy. In the simulation it has
three explicit outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning in the game |
|---|---|
| Standard participation | The fictional party meets the scenario's declared mobility, stewardship, and community-agreement requirements. |
| Exceptional/support pathway | A documented scenario arrangement exists—such as supported transport, co-resident responsibility, a temporary repair/stabilisation plan, or coordinated professional/community support. |
| Different infrastructure/support configuration required | This specific ground configuration cannot provide all of the current material requirements. The simulation exposes other community, housing, care, or support configurations without stigma or abandonment. |

The third outcome is never “no support.” It means this particular ground is not
being misrepresented as universal infrastructure. The player retains agency,
privacy, local ties, and alternative scenario paths.

## Formal-service and community-ground boundary

The game represents a `ServiceBoundary` rather than pretending to deliver a
service it cannot provide. A scenario may expose a fictionalised support option
or a user-initiated link to reviewed real information, but it cannot assess,
admit, refer, prioritise, diagnose, or decide eligibility for real services.

```text
player-selected situation, structural pressures, local ties, and preferences
  → transparent match between material needs and ground capabilities
  ├── standard or exceptional mobility-centred pathway
  └── different community/housing/support configuration option
       (no stigma, no automated referral, no personal-data disclosure)
```

This boundary applies particularly where a person needs clinical care, continuous
supervision, protected accommodation, substantial daily living support, or
another specialised setting. It does not erase those realities from the game; it
prevents the game from falsely promising that peer infrastructure is sufficient.

## Design and governance safeguards

- Participation rules declare their purpose, evidence, exception process, review
  interval, and effect before a scenario begins.
- A ground cannot secretly deny access because of a sensitive profile field.
- Exceptional pathways are finite, resourced, consent-based, and visible; they
  are not informal promises that shift unmanageable responsibility onto peers.
- No violence, coercion, criminalisation, eviction spectacle, or forced
  confinement is a gameplay solution.
- Community members can maintain boundaries and capacity; care/support work is
  recognised, consent-based, and not invisibly imposed on a household.
- Real-world deployment of anything resembling participation/admission criteria
  requires independent legal, disability-access, housing, clinical-safety, human-
  rights, and lived-experience review. The game has no authority to make those
  decisions.

## v0.1 proof

v0.1 contains one mobility-centred ground scenario, one camper-yard readiness
path, one explicit exceptional/support arrangement, and one respectful
different-configuration branch that opens an alternative fictional support setting. All
outcomes must show the underlying scenario rule and must pass tests for no hidden
sensitive-field proxy, no abandonment, no forced disclosure, and no real-service
eligibility claim.
