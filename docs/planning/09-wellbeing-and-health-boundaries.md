# Rolling Commons: Wellbeing, Health Boundaries, and Reflection

## Purpose

Rolling Commons can be a calm, dignified space in which a player explores
housing, travel, community, care, stress, routines, and support choices that may
otherwise be difficult to talk about. It can help a player rehearse possibilities
and recognise that there are multiple paths through a difficult life chapter. It
can also present region-aware, source-linked safety scenarios involving remote
travel, wilderness, water, weather, animals, equipment, communication, and
access to help—so hazards are not made invisible simply because they are serious.

It is **not** therapy, a clinical product, a diagnostic system, treatment,
medical/nutrition advice, crisis service, or substitute for qualified support.
The game must never claim that a simulated action will improve a condition or
tell a person what to do about trauma, anxiety, blood pressure, heart issues, or
another health concern.

## Simulation, rehearsal, and safety learning

The game can represent mental, social, physiological, and environmental pressures
as part of a coherent life simulation. A player can explore how stress, isolation,
care responsibilities, limited transport, poor communications, fatigue, an
existing condition they choose to represent, equipment failure, or a remote
location change the options available to a fictional party.

This is valuable because a safe virtual rehearsal can make issues discussable and
help a player notice questions to take to trusted people or qualified services.
The scenario must show trade-offs, uncertainty, available resources, and limits
of the player's knowledge. It must never tell a player that a simulated outcome
predicts their health or replaces real assessment.

### Source-linked regional hazard scenarios

Region packs may include reviewed hazard cards and decision scenarios: remote
4WD travel and communications, fire/flood disruption, snake or other animal
bites, marine stingers, crocodile habitat, heat, water, swimming, or equipment
failure. Each scenario is a dated game interpretation of an authoritative source,
with geography, season/context, source/version, uncertainty, learning goal, and
content warning recorded in the pack.

The model can represent fictional communication coverage, satellite/emergency
equipment availability, distance/access constraints, companions, first-aid
resource presence, wildlife/hazard warnings, and choices to delay, change route,
seek local assistance, or request emergency help. It must not dynamically triage
a real person, identify a creature/condition from user input, or generate
ungrounded first-aid instructions. Live-help links are one scenario option and
an explicit real-world handoff—not a substitute for planning, preparation, or
the game's learning content.

The Australia-first safety library should cite current official sources per
scenario. For example, Healthdirect identifies snake bites as potentially
life-threatening and Queensland provides location-specific crocodile swimming
guidance. [Healthdirect snake-bite guidance](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/amp/article/snake-bites) · [Queensland crocodile swimming guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/animals/living-with/crocodiles/becrocwise/tips-for-recreational-activities/swimming) · [Healthdirect jellyfish guidance](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/jellyfish-stings)

## Private wellbeing context

Wellbeing is a separate, optional local record connected to—not merged with—a
LifeChapter. A player may choose the level of abstraction: no detail; a general
need such as a quieter pace; or player-authored health/care context. The game
does not require a diagnosis, medical history, clinical value, medication,
pregnancy status, trauma detail, or explanation for a preference.

| Player may choose to model | Simulation can represent | Never infer or claim |
|---|---|---|
| Stress/anxiety sensitivity or trauma-informed preferences | Pacing, sensory intensity, event warnings, private/low-pressure activities, rest/time planning | Diagnosis, cause, severity, therapeutic effect |
| Health/care continuity preference | Time/transport planning, support-network and service-access *scenario* goals, storage/privacy preferences | Medical need, treatment plan, clinical urgency |
| Mobility, fatigue, cardiovascular, or other health-related constraint | Player-selected energy/rest/accessibility budget and alternative task pathways | Symptoms, risk, safe exertion level, health outcome |
| Substance-use concern, recovery, or support boundary | Player-chosen time, money, trust, routine, support, and safety planning constraints | Diagnosis, intoxication level, treatment need, moral worth, a fixed prognosis |
| Body comfort, mobility, food, or body-image concern | Accessibility, comfort, pacing, food/storage, movement, and non-stigmatising social context | A weight score, desirability, willpower, health outcome, dietary prescription |
| Safety/abuse context | Privacy, trusted-contact, separate-resource, boundary, safe-exit, and support-path *scenario* options | Blame, abuse roleplay, a risk prediction, a real safety plan, legal outcome |
| Household/child care responsibility | Time, space, support, consent, and scheduling choices | Parenting suitability, child welfare assessment |

Sensitive context is visible only to the player by default. It is excluded from
town packs, public evidence panels, sharing, telemetry, Trading Post listings,
and LLM context. If a future feature needs a facet—for example, a chosen language
or low-sensory preference—the player grants it separately, for one stated use.

## Consequences without stigma or violence

The simulation must not imply that harmful patterns, unmet needs, equipment
neglect, isolation, coercion, or an absence of support have no effect. Explicit
scenario facts can influence time, money, trust, sleep/rest capacity, project
reliability, access to transport, social connection, energy for tasks, or the
need to pause and seek support. Those effects are always transparent, revisable,
and linked to the declared scenario—not hidden personality penalties.

The counterweight is agency. A player can set a boundary, change pace, choose a
different dwelling, ask a trusted person/community for support, separate
resources, use a safer fictional route, defer a project, access a support/service
scenario option, or reset/rewrite the premise. The game rewards repair,
connection, consent, accountability, and nonviolent problem-solving; it does not
offer violence, abuse, coercive control, humiliation, dangerous substance use,
or self-harm as entertaining/powerful player mechanics.

Substance-use contexts use person-first, non-judgmental language and model harm,
support, boundaries, and recovery as non-linear fictional pathways—not a crime
system or a moral score. Body/weight is never a player stat: only player-selected
comfort, access, mobility, food, and support constraints are relevant. Abuse is
never playable from the perpetrator perspective. A player may choose an abstract
safety/transition scenario, but the game does not store a real safety plan; any
real-world support link is explicit and user-initiated. 1800RESPECT notes that
safety planning is best developed with a specialist domestic, family and sexual
violence support service. [1800RESPECT safety planning](https://1800respect.org.au/help-and-support/safety-planning)

## Therapeutically informed environment, not therapy

The environment may use trauma-informed and accessibility-aware interaction
principles: choice and control, clear expectations, content warnings, no forced
disclosure, adjustable pace/sensory load, pause/exit controls, non-punitive
setbacks, and respectful language. It must not present these as therapeutic
interventions or measure a player's recovery.

The three presentation profiles should all support a calm mode: reduced motion,
lower visual/audio intensity, optional private HUD, predictable navigation, and
the ability to replace stressful scenario content with a neutral planning path.

## Option Explorer

An optional **Option Explorer** can help players think through fictionalised
life-planning alternatives. It uses the game graph to compare, for example:

- a quieter dwelling, shared versus private space, or a demountable home;
- less travel versus a more connected community ground;
- a project split into smaller stages, mentorship, paid assistance, or rest;
- communication, transport, food, energy, care/time, and social-support
  constraints selected by the player.

It returns transparent scenario trade-offs and questions, not a recommendation,
diagnosis, treatment, or risk assessment. Every output identifies the
player-confirmed preferences and the game assumptions it used. A player can
remove a premise and rerun the fictional scenario.

## Agents, rules, and safety

- Agents never receive health/trauma/wellbeing fields by default and cannot
  generate medical, therapeutic, crisis, or ungrounded safety guidance.
- Rules may unlock alternative simulation pathways from explicit accessibility,
  pace, or care/time preferences; they must not gate a person's value, rights,
  housing, community access, or skill potential on a condition.
- A wellbeing field can never be a hidden difficulty multiplier, insurance-like
  score, eligibility proxy, or marketing/telemetry input.
- Content involving trauma, separation, illness, disaster, or children needs
  sensitivity review, warnings, skip routes, and neutral alternatives.
- Substance-use, body/food, and abuse content requires additional lived-
  experience, clinical/safety, and language review. It must not use stigmatising
  labels or equate a circumstance with character or capability.

For an Australia-focused release, any user-initiated “need real help now” link
must point to current official resources and make clear that the game cannot
provide urgent help. It must be static/explicit rather than based on automated
mental-health inference. Healthdirect states that immediate danger is an
emergency requiring triple zero (000), and lists Australian crisis support
options. [Healthdirect mental health crisis support](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/mental-health-crisis-support)

## v0.1 and acceptance

v0.1 includes wellbeing/accessibility preferences plus one reviewed, fictional
regional safety scenario with source/version/provenance and explicit simulation
labels. It includes calm mode, pace, content warnings, optional rest/time
planning, party/care scheduling, no-forced-disclosure controls, and local
deletion/reset. It does not collect or simulate diagnoses, vital signs,
medication, real medical care, or trauma history.

It also includes explicit consequence-and-support fixtures: one deferred/
alternative project pathway, one community-support pathway, and one private
boundary/transition scenario with a neutral exit. It does not simulate substance
consumption, body weight, abuse, violence, or self-harm as player mechanics.

Later exploration of private player-authored health/care context requires a
separate privacy, clinical-safety, legal, accessibility, and lived-experience
review. It must prove field-level consent; complete agent/export exclusion;
neutral/non-stigmatising presentation; no diagnosis/treatment/personalised advice;
source review for safety content; and immediate deletion/reset without changing
unrelated game state.
