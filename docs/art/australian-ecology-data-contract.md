# Australian ecology and roadside fauna data contract

The world model accepts Australian place packs across arbitrary regions. A scene receives a versioned locality profile, not a single national species list. The first profile is the broad corridor the user described: coastal travel from Noosa to Melbourne, inland toward the South Australian border. Its boundary is deliberately still approximate; the game must not portray it as a GIS polygon or one ecologically uniform place.

`assets/ecology/profiles/se-corridor-reference.json` records that scope and the user's roadside context: goats, horses, rabbits, kangaroos, wallabies, wombats, deer, snakes and goannas can be hazards in relevant places; crows may gather at carcasses and kookaburras are roadside fauna. The first scene uses original, illustrative actors on the fictional Kestrel–Saltwind demo road. That combined showcase is not a claim that every animal occurs together at one locality, and its placements are not geolocated sightings. General groups such as kangaroos, wallabies, deer, snakes and goannas remain taxonomically unresolved until a place profile selects local species.

The driving fixture exposes dawn, day, dusk and night. Its twilight presets extend the warning horizon and lower the speed at which a close encounter is classified as high risk; night extends the warning horizon for visibility. These settings change playable warning and lighting state. Place packs can later supply time-aware activity or sighting rules from cited evidence, but the fixture does not infer local wildlife activity from an astronomical clock.

The drive HUD includes clearly labelled simulated oil and water/coolant temperatures, each with a risk colour and rising/cooling trend. Separate road and engine alert lines keep wildlife warnings from obscuring a temperature warning. Ambient temperature, sustained throttle, ascent, rough surfaces, airflow and rig mass affect the simulated readings; the HUD also reports grade, road surface, engine load and condition. Sustained full load in hot weather or on climbs can reach critical temperatures, degrade simulated engine condition and end the drive. Tow-rig traffic queues build when the player travels below the road flow; trailer bodies obscure the centre mirror until a visible road bend or steering input opens the simulated rear view. These are game systems, not vehicle diagnostics or measured road data.

## Import and rendering flow

1. Acquire a dated, bounded public-data snapshot during pack authoring. Keep the original source export out of the browser bundle unless its licence and privacy conditions permit redistribution.
2. Keep region boundaries, vegetation context, occurrences, and road-network data as independent layers with their own CRS, date, licence and transformations. IBRA and NVIS provide context at their published product scales; neither is a substitute for local species observations.
3. Filter ALA occurrence downloads to the place boundary in the public data system. Preserve the download DOI, query, date, attribution, taxonomic identifiers and per-record licence information. Do not expose sensitive coordinates in the game.
4. Run `node scripts/import-ala-ecology.mjs <occurrences.csv> <snapshot-metadata.json> <region-profile.json>`. The importer creates an aggregate review report. It does not generate a local GIS engine or place animal models from coordinates.
5. Review occurrence age, identification, spatial uncertainty, reporting bias, licence and sensitivity. A profile author may then include an eligible modelled species for that place. Keep the evidence status and confidence with the profile.
6. Road-risk placement needs road-specific evidence or an explicitly authored scenario. A species occurrence, distribution polygon or broad regional note alone does not prove that an animal is on a particular road or that collision risk has a particular value. Time-of-day risk must also carry place-specific evidence or an explicit scenario rule; dusk/dawn presentation alone is not an ecological observation.

The stable model catalogue can expand independently of any one region. Profiles select stable string render keys, not a fixed-width species bitmask. Unknown taxa remain in the profile data; they are not silently replaced with a generic animal or plant. The engine now accepts both `faunaSelection` and `floraSelection`; the illustrative corridor profile includes original bottlebrush, banksia, grass-tree, saltbush and kangaroo-paw model groups. Their combined placement is an Australian flora showcase across separate fictional grounds, not a claim that these groups co-occur at one locality. A source-backed local profile must select the groups appropriate to that place. A loaded place may show imported flora/fauna context without creating a polished 3D model for every record. Native and introduced species retain distinct conservation/management relationships where authoritative data supports them. The currently exported HMC snapshot prebuilds the corridor showcase profile; other valid profile selections use the runtime recipe compiler until their profile-specific pack is generated.

## Provenance requirements

Each packaged ecology profile records provider and dataset, retrieval date, version, DOI or stable citation, attribution, licence, geographic scope and boundary version, source CRS and transformations, record filters, taxonomic backbone, uncertainty, sensitivity handling, and importer version/hash. Keep original coordinates and full source exports in the authoring snapshot area, subject to the source terms; the runtime profile contains only reviewed regional summaries and approved presentation selections.

The ALA's API and download workflows are documented at [ALA developer documentation](https://docs.ala.org.au/). ALA downloads can have DOI and attribution requirements, and record licences can vary; the [ALA citation and licensing guidance](https://support.ala.org.au/support/solutions/articles/6000261662-citing-the-ala) and [ALA terms of use](https://www.ala.org.au/terms-of-use/) govern the exact snapshot. Australia's IBRA v7 framework has 89 bioregions and 419 subregions; see [DCCEEW IBRA](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/land/nrs/science/ibra). NVIS products have different dates, resolutions and formats, so select and cite a specific [NVIS data product](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/environment-information-australia/national-vegetation-information-system/data-products) per pack.

The importer requires a region profile and a metadata sidecar. It aggregates only and never edits the supplied CSV or derives exact encounter coordinates.
